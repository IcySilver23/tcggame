## The controls

- **Synthetic lattice:** a drawn 20px diagonal lattice is recovered at 20.03px with r = 0.93 and diag = 0.95. The code can find a lattice when one exists.
- **Flat scan of the same card (swsh45sv SV107):** strongest patch r = 0.33, pitch 21.35% — the same order as the photographs.
- **Photo vs scan:** photo pitch 5.85% median (range 1.10–18.40%), scan pitch 10.86% median (range 3.77–21.35%); photo grain 5px vs scan grain 5px.

## What is sourced and what is drawn

- What the photographs DO add: the surface is visibly granular, not smooth — a sparkle the eye reads as etched. That is an appearance claim, and it is why the finish is drawn as relief.
- What they do not: any repeat. The photo pitches scatter across the whole search range and the flat scan of the same card returns a peak of comparable strength at a different lag; an estimator that reports a period for a diffusely lit scan is reading artwork, not a press.
- Resolution is no longer the limit at the top end: the 3024x4032 English still gives a card about 2700 px wide, where the shipped 3.93% pitch would span roughly 105 px, and it still produces no attributable repeat. The remaining explanation is that this finish has no strong periodic lattice, not that the camera cannot see one.
- So `SURFACE_TEXTURES.etched` keeps `verified: false` and its 18-cycle lattice. For cos(2πN(u±v)): N = 1 / (pitchPerCard × √2). The shipped fine relief uses N = 18, a 3.93% pitch.
