# Shiny Vault grain: can its scale be measured?

Generated by `tmp/measure-shiny-grain.py`; inputs are fetched by `tmp/fetch-shiny-etch.mjs` (not committed).

## Verdict

**The grain scale is NOT attributable: the stills and the flat scan read the same correlation length, and the number moves with the camera resolution rather than with the card, so the relief keeps its modelled density — accepted as final in the Decision section below, not left open.**

The photographs' granular correlation length is 0.675% of card width (median, n=25); the flat catalogue scan's is 0.775% (n=7). The control is not separating.

The reason is resolution, not search: the flat scan is 734 px wide, so its ~0.7% reading is about 5 px - near the pixel scale - while the 3024 px still reads 0.29-0.43% (8-11 px). A length that changes with the camera is not a property of the card.

## Decision: the modelled grain is final

**Accepted on 2026-11-07.** It cannot be measured from the evidence available here, so the drawn density is final for this build.

The question was: *How coarse is the Shiny Vault grain?*

Four tests, two extrapolation models and two patch selections all fail to converge, and two still return a negative length. A number that moves that far between photographs of the same card is a property of the camera and the JPEG, not of the paper, so no measurement can be preferred to the drawing.

| Test | What it does | What it found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| resolution ladder | the same 3024px still rendered down to 500px and measured at each width | L50 climbs 0.36% at 2621px to 0.97% at 734px to 1.29% at 500px, and the 734px flat scan reads 0.71% at the same width: the length tracks pixel size, not the card |
| independent stills, linear intercept | four ladders extrapolated to zero pixel size | 0.162%, 0.085%, -0.290%, 0.532% — spread 0.82 percentage points |
| quadrature model | L50^2 = L^2 + (k x pixel)^2, which cannot return a negative length | 0.365%, 0.284%, 0.000%, 0.709% — spread 0.71 percentage points |
| flat patches only | each source re-measured on its four least detailed 16%-of-card regions | linear 0.073%, -0.246%, 0.085%, -0.014% (spread 0.33 pp); quadrature 0.163%, 0.000%, 0.300%, 0.000% (spread 0.30 pp) |

The shipped density is three octaves at periods 48/96/192 on the 512 sheet — cells of 2.08%, 1.04% and 0.52% of card width — at a 0.34 relief strength.

What follows from the decision:

- `sparkleHeight` keeps those periods and that strength; the decision changes no code, only what the build says about them.
- The `sparkle` surface note and AGENTS.md call the density accepted, not pending, so a later reader does not reopen the measurement without new evidence.
- The finish stays relief, not a paper: `shiny-db.ts` keeps the pattern `special-treatment` and no sheet is borrowed.
- No player-visible change follows: 0.58.0 already shipped this surface.

**What would reopen it:** A focused stack, or a macro with a known scale reference plus a SMOOTH card captured at the same magnification and resolution. Only then can a ladder converge on a length that belongs to the card; the existing photographs cannot, however they are fitted.

## Extrapolating to infinite resolution

The still ladder extrapolates to 0.162% of card width and the scan to 0.085%, but the independent stills break the claim: their ladders intercept at -0.290%, 0.532%, a spread of 0.82 percentage points. An intercept that moves that far between photographs of the same card is not a property of the card, so no true grain length is recorded and the density stays modelled by decision (see "Decision" above).

Fitting `L50 (% card) = a + b x pixel size (% card)`, where pixel size is `100 / card width`:

| source | intercept a (% card) | slope b | R² | implied cell (% card) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3024px still | 0.162% | 5.78 | 0.997 | below the 0.39% calibration floor |
| flat scan | 0.085% | 4.55 | 1.000 | below the 0.39% calibration floor |

The shipped `sparkle` octaves are cells of 0.52%, 1.04% and 2.08% of card width. With the intercepts scattering, that table is orientation only: no density follows from it, in either direction.

Two caveats keep this out of the constants: an intercept extrapolates past the measured range, and the synthetic curve only believes `L50` down to a 2 px cell on a 512 sheet — a finer grain cannot be represented without a larger sheet.

## What is drawn, and why it is not tuned

- The shipped `sparkle` relief uses three octaves at periods 48/96/192 on the 512 sheet, i.e. cells of about 2.1%, 1.07% and 0.53% of the card width. That density is a drawing.
- Nothing here justifies moving it. A fine photo reads 0.29–0.43% and a coarse one 0.7%, and the flat scan reads the same as the photographs; picking a number from this table would be a choice dressed as a measurement.
- What would settle it: a focused stack or a macro photograph with a known scale reference, where the pixel size is well below the grain, plus a smooth control at the SAME resolution.

## The search for a scaled macro

**No photograph with a scale bar was found.** The card is already a scale — the 3024 px still puts 2621 px across a 63 mm card, 41.6 px/mm — so the missing piece is not a ruler but a *smooth* card at the same magnification and resolution.

| Channel | What came back |
| --- | --- |
| Bing image search | 11 queries for ruler / scale bar / microscope / 1200 dpi / texture-comparison frames; returns stock rulers, card-size guides, CT scans of a Base Set Charizard and flat listings, but no scaled macro of a Shiny Vault card |
| Yahoo!オークション | listings are phone photographs with no scale bar and no smooth card at the same distance |
| elitefourum / PokéBeach site searches | no texture thread surfaced with a scaled macro; the images returned were unrelated textures |
| YouTube (yt-dlp) | the nearest Shiny Vault showcase tops out at 720p; no 4K raking-light macro |

- **Already have:** the card itself is a scale: the 3024x4032 still measures 2621 px across a 63 mm card, 41.6 px/mm, which is how L50 is quoted in mm.
- **Missing:** a SMOOTH card photographed at the same magnification and resolution in the same frame (or the same focus stack); without it the control keeps reading at its own pixel scale, which is the confound.
- **Consequence:** the grain density stays modelled — accepted as final by the decision below — and `sparkleHeight` keeps its 48/96/192 periods.
